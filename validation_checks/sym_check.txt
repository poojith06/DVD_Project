RESULT
* (a) is not symmetric; (b) is exactly symmetric (mirror error 0.00e+00 for every row and every current, NMOS OFF and
  PMOS OFF); (c) geomod = 0 is also exactly symmetric.  => The asymmetry is caused by the default junction geometry of
  the instance: with geomod = 1 and no AD/AS/PD/PS given, BSIM4 does not treat the drain and source junctions
  identically. (ngspice reports the instance values ad = as = pd = ps = 0 in both cases; the unequal values are the
  effective geometry BSIM4 derives internally from geomod, which was not printed - the evidence above is the swap
  test, not a read-out of those internal values.) The hypothesis is confirmed.
* The asymmetry only matters for tiny currents. In the random Part A rows of variant (a), the rows with a mirror error
  above 1 % all have a dominant current of at most 7.1e-12 A (NMOS OFF: 3 of 15 rows, 4.8e-13 .. 7.1e-12 A) and at most
  2.2e-12 A (PMOS OFF: 7 of 15 rows, 5.7e-15 .. 2.2e-12 A); the remaining rows have mirror errors below 1 % (maximum
  6.9e-03 and 9.1e-03). Where the junction-leakage floor (about 1e-14 A) is a large part of the current, the two
  orientations differ; in the rows with larger leakage they agree to about 1 %.
* At the gate operating points (one terminal at each rail) the unmodified device (a) is symmetric to 2e-05 (NMOS OFF) and
  4.6e-05 (PMOS OFF) at most over 20 corners (median 0), i.e. about 0.002 % and 0.005 %. Giving the instance an
  explicit equal geometry changes the dominant current there by at most 2.4e-05 (NMOS OFF) and 1.4e-05 (PMOS OFF)
  relative. For gate leakage the asymmetry is therefore irrelevant.
* Consequence for the project: all data (Part A, Part B, Part C) were generated with the same default geometry (no
  AD/AS/PD/PS on any instance), so they are consistent with each other. Terminals are used as simulated (no symmetry
  preprocessing), because the training rows contain this orientation dependence for small currents. The equal-geometry
  variant (b) is analysis only and was not used to generate any data.
